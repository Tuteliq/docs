# ScamLingua independent evaluation (Phase 1)

In October 2026, Carl Lochstampfor of ScamLingua tested five of Tuteliq's text detections from the outside, using test conversations that he wrote and that Tuteliq had not seen. This page explains what was tested and what was found, including where Tuteliq fell short. It is a summary. If anything here differs from the reports, the reports are correct.

<Note>
**Correction issued on October 28, 2026.** After the reports were published, ScamLingua issued an erratum that corrects the social engineering figures. This page uses the corrected figures. See [The social engineering correction](#the-social-engineering-correction) below.
</Note>

## Who ran it

- **Lead evaluator:** Carl Lochstampfor, an undergraduate student in cybersecurity at Old Dominion University. ScamLingua is his research project on detecting scams and online harm in conversations.
- **Second evaluator:** Dr. Ayan Roy of Christopher Newport University, who checked a sample of the test labels "blind", meaning without seeing which answer was intended.
- **Independence:** Carl Lochstampfor designed, ran and scored the evaluation. Tuteliq gave access to its evaluation harness (the tool Tuteliq uses to run outside evaluators' test sets through its API) and reviewed the reports for factual accuracy only. Tuteliq had no role in the test design, the scoring or the conclusions, and did not see any test conversation before the test sets were fixed.
- **Funding:** the evaluation took more than 175 hours from September 25, 2026, and was unpaid. Tuteliq has not paid for it or for any of its content.
- **Purpose:** the full report states that it supports Tuteliq's EIC application.
- **What it is not:** the reports state that this is not an accreditation, certification or audit of Tuteliq.

### How this relates to COVA-X

COVA-X is a separate ScamLingua dataset of approximately 11,000 synthetic scam conversations. This evaluation did **not** use COVA-X. Its test conversations were written new for this evaluation, across five harm categories. The reports describe the evaluation as building on earlier work auditing and refining the COVA and COVA-X datasets.

## What was tested

This was a **black-box** evaluation: it tested how Tuteliq behaves from the outside, with no access to how it works inside. The version tested was Tuteliq API 3.4.0, detector `tuteliq-detect-v5.20261015`, on October 19, 2026.

Five of Tuteliq's text detections were tested on 1,294 conversations, about half harmful and half harmless, plus 50 single messages:

| Category | Conversations | Harmful | Harmless |
|---|---|---|---|
| Romance scam | 260 | 130 | 130 |
| Social engineering | 256 | 130 | 126 |
| Coercive control | 260 | 130 | 130 |
| Bullying | 259 | 129 | 130 |
| Self-harm and emotional distress | 259 | 129 | 130 |

These are the counts in the original reports. The erratum later moved five social engineering conversations from harmless to harmful (see [below](#the-social-engineering-correction)).

**Not tested:** grooming, money-mule recruitment and Tuteliq's other detections; languages other than English; and images, audio and video. No result here covers them. No child sexual abuse material was used under any circumstances.

### How the test conversations were made

All conversations are **synthetic**: text-message exchanges written by an AI language model (Gemma 3 12B) running on the evaluator's own computers. No real person's messages were used. Each harmful pattern is based on published research or official guidance.

Each category has five kinds of conversation:

- **Baseline harmful:** the harm stated plainly.
- **Evasion probes:** the same harm, hidden or softened.
- **Adversarial probes:** the same harm, disguised as something legitimate.
- **Baseline harmless:** ordinary conversations of the same setup and length.
- **Hard negatives:** harmless conversations that look suspicious on the surface.

Before testing, each set was checked and read by hand, then "frozen": every file was fingerprinted so that any later change would show.

### How results were scored

Tuteliq's answer for each conversation includes a recommended action. From lowest to highest these are: none, monitor, flag for review, block, and immediate intervention. The evaluation counted a harmful conversation as **detected** when Tuteliq recommended an action above "monitor", that is, flag for review or stronger.

Two measures matter most:

- **Detection rate** (the report calls it recall): of the harmful conversations, the share Tuteliq detected.
- **False alarm rate:** of the harmless conversations, the share Tuteliq wrongly flagged.

The reports also give a 95% range for every figure, because the test sets are small. A wide range means a figure is less certain. The full report has these ranges.

## Results

Every figure in this section comes with this qualifier, which the reports require:

> "In an independent evaluation by Carl Lochstampfor on synthetic, English-language text conversations (detector tuteliq-detect-v5.20261015, tested October 19, 2026). Not an accreditation, certification or audit."

All 1,344 test cases got a usable answer, and none failed.

| Category | Harmful conversations detected | Harmless conversations wrongly flagged |
|---|---|---|
| Romance scam | 100% (130/130) | 6.9% (9/130) |
| Coercive control | 99.2% (129/130) | 0.8% (1/130) |
| Self-harm and emotional distress | 96.1% (124/129) | 0.8% (1/130) |
| Bullying | 84.5% (109/129) | 4.6% (6/130) |
| Social engineering (corrected, v1.1) | 83.7% (113/135) | 13.2% (16/121) |

Social engineering was the weakest category, with the lowest detection and the most false alarms.

**Single messages.** When 50 key messages were sent alone, without the rest of the conversation, 20 of 25 harmful messages were detected and 1 of 25 harmless messages was flagged. With only five per category, the report says these figures show direction only.

**Second evaluator.** Dr. Ayan Roy labelled 50 conversations blind and agreed with the evaluator's labels on 41 (82%; Cohen's kappa 0.64). Cohen's kappa measures agreement between two people while allowing for agreement that could happen by chance. Most disagreements were harmless conversations built to look suspicious, which he read as harmful. In all nine disagreements, Tuteliq's result matched the evaluator's label, so the report says they do not inflate Tuteliq's error counts.

## Strengths the report found

- **Romance scams, coercive control and self-harm** were detected in 96% to 100% of harmful conversations, with false alarms at or below 6.9%.
- **Detection held up on the synthetic evasion and adversarial probes.** Detection on these hidden or disguised versions stayed close to detection on the plain versions in every category. As the report puts it: "Probes designed by the evaluator; evasion in real conversations may differ." The largest drop was in bullying, from 87.2% on plain conversations to 76.0% on evasion probes.
- **Whole conversations helped** in coercive control and self-harm, where the whole conversation was detected more often than its key message alone.

## Weaknesses the report found

- **Social engineering** had the lowest detection rate and the highest false alarm rate (corrected: 83.7% detected, 13.2% false alarms).
- **Scams asking for identity data under a government pretext** were mostly missed: 7 of 28 detected (25.0%). Harmless conversations of the same kind were flagged as often (7 of 28). The report says Tuteliq does not yet tell a scam asking for identity data apart from a genuine identity check. These figures come from the original v1.0 report; the erratum did not restate them.
- **Social engineering false alarms** in the original report were mostly on routine account-security and tech-support contact.
- **Bullying that builds up gradually** from teasing was detected in 65.2% of cases (30 of 46), against over 90% for open exclusion or exposure. Mockery of someone's abilities was detected in 45.5% of cases (10 of 22).
- **Romance false alarms** (6.9%) came mostly from harmless conversations about money between people who trust each other. Genuine loan requests between such people were flagged in 8 of 15 cases. Harmless requests to split a cost (0 of 38) or pay someone back (0 of 22) were never flagged.
- **The "monitor" level is applied to many harmless conversations.** When any action counts, including monitor, Tuteliq placed 56.9% of harmless romance conversations, 53.2% of harmless social engineering conversations (original v1.0 figure) and 25.4% of harmless coercive control conversations at monitor or above. The report recommends that platforms tie automatic action to "flag for review" and above, not to "monitor".

The report makes five recommendations to Tuteliq, covering identity-data requests under a government pretext, social engineering false alarms, loans between people who trust each other, bullying that escalates gradually, and guidance on the monitor level.

## Limits of this evaluation

The reports list these limits. The results describe this detector version on these synthetic conversations, not performance on real platform traffic.

- **Synthetic data.** All conversations come from one AI model. Real conversations are messier and more varied.
- **Short conversations.** The longest test conversations run 26 turns. Real romance scams can run to over a thousand lines across weeks.
- **Small subgroups.** Each category has about 130 harmful and 130 harmless conversations. Subgroups within a category are smaller, so their figures are less certain.
- **Ages not stated.** The conversations do not say how old the speakers are, so the results describe detection in general, not for children specifically.
- **English text only.** Other languages, other harm categories, images, audio and video were not tested.
- **Real platforms have less harm.** About half the test conversations were harmful. On real platforms, harm is rare, so a much larger share of flags would be false alarms than this test mix suggests. The report treats detection rate and false alarm rate as the figures to compare.
- **No compliance assessment.** The evaluation does not assess or validate regulatory compliance by Tuteliq or any platform.
- **Future retests need new data.** After the evaluation, Tuteliq was given royalty-free permission to use the test conversations for internal testing and regression checks. The report says any future independent retest of Tuteliq should therefore use new, unseen conversations.

## The social engineering correction

On October 28, 2026, ScamLingua published an erratum (version 1.1). A label review of the social engineering category, prompted by questions from Tuteliq's team, found that 5 of the 126 conversations labelled harmless contained requests that harmless conversations were designed not to include, such as a link to gain account access or a request to confirm a security code. These should have been labelled social engineering. Two more were borderline.

| Social engineering | Detected | False alarms |
|---|---|---|
| Published (v1.0) | 83.8% (109/130) | 15.9% (20/126) |
| Corrected (v1.1) | 83.7% (113/135) | 13.2% (16/121) |
| Range including 2 borderline | 83.2% (114/137) | 12.6% (15/119) |

The erratum states that all other categories and figures are unchanged, that social engineering remains the weakest category, and that the reports' conclusions and recommendations stand.

## How to quote these results

The reports set rules for quoting their figures. In short:

- Always include the qualifier shown under [Results](#results).
- Quote a category's detection rate only together with that category's false alarm rate.
- Do not combine the categories into one overall detection or accuracy rate.
- Do not make claims about real platform traffic, other languages, images, audio or video, or Tuteliq's other detections.
- Do not describe the evaluation as a certification, accreditation, audit or compliance assessment.
- Do not quote the strengths without the weaknesses.

Use the corrected social engineering figures from the erratum. The full rules are in the claims register at the end of each report.
